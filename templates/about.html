Brief Summary

Project Title: Development and Deployment of Scalable Speaker Recognition Technology in Different Applications

Institutes:KLETech University Hubballi

Investigators: Satish Chikmath

Startup: To be identified through open call and also through CoE, IIT Madras

Deliverables

  1. Speaker recognition databases for Indian context i.e., multilingual, multi dialect, multi accent, multi sensor, multi-channel, multi environment and limited data conditions. (1000 hrs with at least 1000 speakers from multilingual situation of India)
  2. A speaker recognition system that works well for Indian settings.
  3. Voice OTP based person authentication.
  4. Voice based person identification application for call center scenario.
  5. Forensic speaker identification and verification.
  6. Speaker recognition system on embedded platform.

Motivation:

Speaker recognition focuses on recognizing speakers using the speaker-specific information present in the speech signal. The speaker recognition technology may be broadly divided into speaker verification and speaker identification. The goal of speaker verification is to validate the identity claim of a speaker. It is a binary classification task of either accepting or rejecting the identity claim. The goal of speaker identification is to identify the speaker of the given speech signal.

Speaker identification can be built in closed-set, open-set or verification through identification frameworks. In closed-set conditions the identification is only among the known set of speakers of a group.

The applications of speaker verification and identification also depend on the framework chosen. Speaker verification is mainly used for person authentication using speech as biometric. Closed-set case is for scenarios where it is always used by a set of speakers.

In the case of open-set, any attempt by an unknown speaker needs to be thwarted. Verification through identification will help in validating identity claims and therefore the framework must be selected based on application requirements.

Speaker recognition technologies currently deployed in India are mostly borrowed from other regions. Although research literature shows good performance, these systems often fail in practical Indian scenarios.

India has multilingual, multi-dialect, multi-accent and noisy environments. These conditions make speaker recognition more challenging and therefore technology must be developed keeping Indian operating conditions in mind.

Methodology:

The work aims at Development and Deployment of Scalable Speaker Recognition Technology in Different Applications by a consortium of academic institutions, industry and startups. The speaker recognition technology has matured enough, i.e., approaches for verifying or identifying speakers which can be used for deployment.

However, we need to address the practical issues while deploying in an Indian setting. The speaker recognition technology may become the next big bottleneck as increasingly more and more speech mode applications are being deployed in practice.

These issues need to be addressed by collecting data, studying the issues, developing technological wrappers to handle them, performing field study, fine tuning and then deploying.

The academic groups will take care of literature review, exploration, implementation and refinement of technologies based on the needs from industry and startups. Based on this, patents and publications will be generated.

The field requirements and practical challenges will be brought to the table by startups, industry and user agencies. The academic groups will refine and tune the technologies to meet these requirements and challenges.

The startups, industry and user agencies will help in speech data collection, conduct field trials and give feedback. After this, transfer of technology will be made to startups, industry and user agencies.

Speaker Recognition & Applications:

The applications of speaker verification and speaker identification include person authentication services, forensic speaker identification, access control, voice dialing, mobile banking, speaker labeling of meeting recordings, and personalized caller identification.

Two variants of technology are required, namely lightweight and deep models. The lightweight model is intended for deployment on edge devices and nearly real-time operation. The deep model can be used in client-server architectures where real-time operation may not be strict but security and accuracy are important.

Traditional kernels such as GMM-UBM and i-vector are used for lightweight systems, while x-vector and other deep learning approaches are used for deep models. These kernels will be wrapped with additional processing layers to make them usable in different operating conditions.

The goal is to understand user requirements and develop scalable speaker recognition technologies suitable for Indian operational environments.

The consortium will focus on three major technology applications:

• Voice-based OTP for person authentication
• Voice-based person identification in call-center scenarios
• Speaker identification in forensic investigations

Voice-based OTP will enable remote authentication for services such as identity verification. Voice identification in call centers will automatically identify customers before providing services. Forensic speaker identification will help match voices from recordings with suspects during investigations.

The approach is to collaborate with user agencies and industries from the beginning of the project so that technologies can be deployed effectively and scaled for real-world Indian conditions.
